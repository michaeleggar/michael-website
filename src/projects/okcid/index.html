The design started with their story. I worked closely with Julia, our content marketing manager, to understand who the district is to each of those audiences, and the look grew out of that. It’s technical and scientific, but also artsy and colorful, with lots of white space, big bold type, and three main colors with tints that shift depending on which part of the site you’re on. Their logo has a watercolor texture, so I carried that onto the calls to action, used sparingly.

On the development side, this was the first time I built my own custom Elementor widgets. The site is made from more than twenty of them written from scratch, with ACF handling the post types. I also put a lot of care into connecting the directory to the district map. Their team adds an organization with its address and information, and when they save, a script looks up its coordinates in the background. Checking a box on the post places it on the custom MapLibre map, with a pin that shows that organization’s details. Events got the same considered treatment. They filter by date with a custom flatpickr calendar, and an Add to Calendar button builds a calendar file straight from each event post.
