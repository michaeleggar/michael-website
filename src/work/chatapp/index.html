A prop that had to hold up on camera

The film needed a teen cancer support group’s chat open on an actor’s laptop. It had to read as a real app on camera, take whatever the actor typed, and let the crew play every other member of the group from off screen.

Two windows

The on-camera view lets the actor type and send messages, and the logged-in user can be swapped to show the conversation from different characters’ perspectives. A separate crew interface handles everything else: adding and editing users, changing online status, sending messages as other users, saving templated messages for reshoots, and switching between direct messages and video chats.

Try it below. Cue a line from the crew’s side and watch it arrive on the actor’s screen, or reply as Sadie.

Iterating on set

After the first shoot day, the crew needed a way to reshoot without manually retyping the script. They also needed the ability to reorder and edit messages already sent in the chat. I added a template system with a configurable typing-indicator delay and a custom right-click menu for editing messages. Between takes, one reset clears the conversation so the scene can start again from the top.

Rebuilding it in React

The original codebase grew organically under production pressure. After the shoot I rebuilt it with React and Vite. One reducer now drives both windows and keeps them in sync over BroadcastChannel. The rebuild adds chat rooms, a slide-out settings panel that can pop out into its own window, and a layout that works down to phone size.

Switch the demo above to Rebuild to try it. Saved messages still send with a typing delay, and the whole crew side is also tucked behind the cog next to Sadie’s name.
